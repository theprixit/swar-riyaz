How to tune
  • Strike the dayan with a clean Na or Tin on the kinar, not on the syahi. Let it ring. The reading settles about half a second after the stroke.
  • Go around the drum: strike beside each gatta in turn. Every position should read the same. A spot that reads flat needs that gatta pushed down; sharp, that gatta up.
  • Coarse tuning: tap the gatta down with the hammer to raise, up from below to lower. Fine tuning: tap the gajra (the braid) just above the gatta, down to raise, up to lower.
  • Get all positions within about 5 cents, then check the open Tun in the middle; it should match too.
  • Baya is tuned loosely, an octave below Sa or to lower Pa. Read it with a soft open Ghe, heel off the skin.
  • The needle and the trace follow the sound continuously; a vertical line marks each stroke and the verdict is taken from the first second after it; the hint line shows how far it moved since the previous stroke. While the reference tone is sounding the mic is ignored, so with the loop on, strike in the silent gap. Note: so the dayan reading as 281 Hz against a 140.6 Hz Sa is in tune, one octave up, which is normal.
